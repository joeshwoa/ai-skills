# Ideation — when there is no concept yet

Don't jump to intake when the user has only a goal. Propose the video.

1. **Three questions only:** what are we promoting or telling, who is it
   for, what should they do or feel afterwards.
2. **Research before inventing.** Search the web / YouTube / the
   platform for what currently works in that niche, region and season;
   note hooks, lengths, pacing, formats. Say in one line what was
   checked. For Egyptian / MENA audiences build in local dialect,
   humour, settings and the season (Ramadan, exams, summer, Eid, back to
   school) — generic "global" content underperforms locally.
3. **6–8 concepts as a table**, structurally different from each other:

| # | Concept | Hook (first 1.5 s — a concrete image, not a theme) | Format | Shots × length | Route | Est. credits | Why it lands |

4. Offer the next layer: 3 alternative hooks for the chosen concept, a
   6–8 s cutdown, or a 3-part series.

## Formats worth proposing unprompted

- **Character series** — the same saved Flow characters and voices across
  episodes: the identity work is paid once, every episode is cheaper.
- **Question → reveal** (educational): a character asks, the world
  answers visually. Strong for kids' science content.
- **POV / one-take walkthrough** — one continuous camera path, extended.
- **Before / after** and **transformation**.
- **Plate-shoot injection** — the user films something ordinary, Omni
  video-to-video adds the impossible element; post as original vs edited.
- **Talking head + B-roll** — one presenter shot, generated cutaways.

## Hooks that work

A concrete visual in motion in the first second; a question the viewer
wants answered; a face reacting; something impossible; the result shown
first. Never a logo, a title card, or a slow establishing shot first on
social.
