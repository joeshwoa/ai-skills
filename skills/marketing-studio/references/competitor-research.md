# Competitor research

Purpose: research competitors and alternatives from public sources only, within legal and ethical limits, and turn findings into a decision-ready matrix.
Last updated: 2026-10-19

Output feeds `positioning-and-messaging.md` (alternatives map), `funnel-and-channels.md` (channel choice), `content-and-seo.md` (gap analysis) and `templates/competitor-matrix.md`. For a full competitive brief, `marketing:competitive-brief` can be used if installed.

## 1. Legal and ethical limits
Do:
- Use **public** information: websites, public social profiles, public ad libraries, public reviews, marketplaces, press, public filings, job posts, app store pages, Google Maps listings, public price lists.
- Buy or sign up as yourself when testing a competitor's product or funnel, and say who you are if asked. Mystery shopping is common practice, but never use it to extract confidential information or to damage the business.
- Record the source, date and screenshot for every claim.
Do not:
- Scrape behind logins, bypass paywalls, use someone else's account, or break a site's terms or technical controls. Use official APIs and official tools.
- Pose as a customer, employee, journalist or investor to obtain non-public data; fake accounts; deceptive messages.
- Access confidential material (leaked files, internal documents, private groups under false identity).
- Post fake reviews of competitors or paid negative content (this is also a platform violation, for example on Google Business Profile).
- Copy their copy, images or creative; get inspiration from patterns, not assets. Respect trademarks and copyright.
- Make comparative claims that are inaccurate. Check platform rules and local law; where unsure ask a lawyer (not legal advice).
Personal data: collect business information, not private individuals' data; do not store customer data you encounter.

## 2. Method (5 steps)
1. **Define the question**: pricing decision, positioning gap, channel choice, content plan, launch response. Fix the decision first so research stops when it is answerable.
2. **List the set**: 3 direct competitors, 2 substitutes, 1 do-it-yourself/marketplace alternative, and one "best in class" from another market for ideas.
3. **Collect** with the sources in section 3 into the matrix; time-box to a few hours per competitor.
4. **Synthesize**: where do they win, where are they weak, what do customers complain about, what do they all say (parity claims), what nobody says.
5. **Decide and record**: positioning move, offer changes, channels, content topics, price stance; set a re-check date (quarterly or after a competitor's launch).

## 4. SWOT and matrix templates
- **Competitor matrix** (`templates/competitor-matrix.md`): rows = criteria customers use (price, quality, speed, trust, service, range, brand), plus claims, proof, channels, offer; columns = you and each competitor. Score 1 to 5 with a source link per cell. Mark "unknown" instead of guessing.
- **SWOT per competitor**: strengths and weaknesses (internal, observable), opportunities and threats (external, for you). Keep each to 3 bullets with evidence; weaknesses must come from customer reviews or observed gaps, not hope.
- **Messaging map**: their headline claim, proof type, tone, audience cues; mark which claims are parity (everyone says it) and which are unique.
- **Battlecard (for sales/chat)**: when customers compare us to X, say Y; their strengths we respect; where we win; the proof to show; the question to ask the customer.

## 5. Review mining
- Sources: Google Maps reviews, Facebook recommendations, marketplace ratings, app store reviews, Trustpilot-type sites (if they exist for your market), Reddit/Facebook groups (public posts), YouTube comments.
- Sample 50 to 100 reviews per competitor (recent first, mix 1-2 star and 4-5 star). Code each by theme: price, quality, delivery, service, trust, returns, specific features.
- Output: top 5 praised and top 5 criticised themes with quote snippets in the customer's own language (dialect), the jobs customers mention, the objections you can answer, and the promises you can credibly make.
- Cautions: fake and incentivised reviews exist; weigh detailed reviews with specifics; do not treat star averages as comparable across platforms; do not quote private individuals by name.

## 6. Pricing comparison
- Compare like with like: same size/spec, same delivery terms, same warranty, same payment method (COD fee, prepaid discount).
- Capture list price, promotions and frequency, bundles, shipping costs, payment options, guarantees, and price on marketplaces vs own site.
- Compute total cost to the customer and value metrics (price per unit, per use).
- Decide a stance: premium with proof, parity with a better experience, or low-cost with a leaner model. Check margin before any price cut.
- Record dates; prices change quickly.

## 7. SEO and content gaps
- List the 20 queries that matter to your offer; for each, note who ranks, page type, depth, freshness, and what the page misses (answer, proof, local info, Arabic version, video).
- Compare topic coverage: topics competitors rank for that you lack; topics nobody covers well (opportunity).
- Check their site structure (service/location pages, FAQ, blog categories), internal links, and the languages they serve (Arabic/English parity).
- Look at their top shared or commented content on social to find formats the audience likes.
- Gap priority = business value x ease x how weak existing results are. Brief the content plan per `content-and-seo.md`.
- Tools: Search Console for your own data; keyword/SEO tools for estimates (estimates, not truth); manual searches in Arabic and English, incognito and from the target city when possible.

## 8. Share-of-voice proxies
True share of voice needs paid data; use proxies and label them proxies.
- **Search**: share of top-10 results for your 20 queries across competitors.
- **Social**: public follower counts are weak; better use average views or engagements per post on recent posts, posting frequency, and number of creators mentioning each brand.
- **Ads**: number of active ads and creative variants visible in ad libraries; number of formats and landing pages.
- **Reviews**: review counts and recent review velocity on Google/marketplaces.
- **Branded search interest**: relative trend over time in public trend tools.
- **Mentions**: count of public mentions in a set window (manual or social listening).
Report each as relative to the set, with the date and method; never present a proxy as market share.

## 9. Output format
One to two pages (or a slide), in this order:
1. **Question and decision** the research serves.
2. **Set** studied and sources/dates.
3. **Matrix** (table) with scored criteria and links.
4. **Findings**: where they win, where they are weak, parity claims, white space.
5. **Customer language** snippets (dialect), objections, jobs.
6. **Implications**: positioning move, offer, channel, content topics, pricing stance, what to avoid copying.
7. **Confidence and gaps**: unknowns and what to verify.
8. **Re-check date** and triggers (price change, launch, new entrant).
Label every number from a competitor as "observed on [date], source [link]".
