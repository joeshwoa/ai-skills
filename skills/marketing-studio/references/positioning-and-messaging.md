# Positioning and messaging

Purpose: decide who the offer is for, why it wins against alternatives, and the words and proof that carry that claim in English and Egyptian Arabic.
Last updated: 2026-10-19

Output: a positioning statement, a messaging house (`templates/messaging-house.md`), an objection list, and a proof plan. Voice and tone are in `brand-voice.md`; competitor inputs in `competitor-research.md`.

## 1. Jobs to be done (JTBD)
People "hire" a product to make progress in a situation. Interview 5 to 10 recent buyers (and 3 who chose a competitor or did nothing) and capture:
- **Situation/trigger**: what happened right before they looked? ("Ramadan is in three weeks", "the old AC died in July", "my boss asked for a report in English").
- **Job statement**: "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome]."
- **Forces**: push (pain with the current way), pull (attraction of the new), anxiety (fear of the new: price, trust, delivery), habit (inertia). Anxiety and habit are where most Egyptian COD, trust and "will it really arrive" objections live.
- **Functional, emotional, social** dimensions: what it does, how they want to feel, how they want to be seen.
- Verbatim phrases. Save the exact words in dialect; these become headlines and ad hooks.
Rule: no interviews possible? Mine WhatsApp chats, reviews of competitors and comment sections, and mark the result "assumption, verify".

## 2. Alternatives map
The real competitor is whatever the customer does instead.
| Alternative type | Examples | What you must beat |
|---|---|---|
| Direct competitor | Another brand/shop/clinic | Specific advantage on the main buying criterion |
| Substitute | Different product doing the same job | The job done better, cheaper or faster |
| DIY / workaround | Spreadsheet, a cousin who "knows someone", WhatsApp groups | Effort and risk |
| Marketplace / aggregator | Online marketplaces, Facebook groups | Convenience vs trust and price |
| Do nothing | Postpone the purchase | Urgency that is true, risk reversal |
Map each on 2 axes that customers care about (for example price vs. speed, or quality vs. trust) and find an uncontested corner you can credibly own. If every corner is crowded, differentiate on a segment, a proof asset or an experience, not on a slogan.

## 3. Value propositions
Test each claim with four filters: **Relevant** (does the segment care?), **Different** (do alternatives say it?), **Provable** (can we show evidence now?), **Specific** (a number, a time, a condition, not "best quality").
Weak: "High quality, great service." Stronger: "Delivered in Cairo within 48 hours or the delivery fee is refunded" (only if operations can honor it).
Structure: [Outcome the customer wants] + [mechanism/why it works] + [proof] + [risk reduction].
Keep one primary promise. Three supporting pillars is the limit people remember.

## 4. Proof hierarchy (strongest first)
1. **Measured results you own** with method and date (before/after with consent, delivery times, repeat rate).
2. **Customer evidence**: named or consented testimonials, photos, videos, reviews on third-party platforms (Google, Facebook), case studies with context.
3. **Third-party validation**: certifications, licenses, awards, press, partner badges (verify they are current and quotable).
4. **Demonstration**: live demos, trials, samples, workshop/lab/behind-the-scenes footage, a visit to the shop.
5. **Expertise signals**: who makes it, credentials, years, process.
6. **Assertions**: "we are the best" (weakest; use only with the above).
Rules: never fabricate or buy testimonials or reviews; label sponsored or incentivized content; get written permission for names and photos; keep before/after honest (same conditions, no misleading edits); do not claim certifications you do not hold. For health, finance and education, check sector rules before claiming results (see `../../media-buying/references/mena-and-egypt-notes.md`, section 8; not legal advice).

## 5. The messaging house
Fill `templates/messaging-house.md`:
- **Roof**: the single promise (customer-benefit sentence, under 12 words).
- **Pillars (3)**: each a benefit + the mechanism that delivers it.
- **Proof under each pillar**: 1 to 3 assets from section 4.
- **Foundation**: brand voice traits, values, boundaries (what we never say).
- **Per-segment variants**: same house, different first pillar and words.
- **Per-stage lines**: awareness (problem/curiosity), consideration (comparison/proof), decision (offer/risk reversal), retention (usage tips/community).
Check: remove the brand name; could a competitor say the same sentence? If yes, rewrite.

## 6. Pricing and offer angles
These are framing choices that stay honest. Prices must match across ads, page, chat and invoice.
- **Anchoring**: show a reference that is real: the price of the alternative (clinic visit, imported model), the full price of a bundle if items are sold separately, or the cost per day/use. Never invent a "was" price or show a price you never charged; many platforms and consumer rules treat this as deceptive (check local rules; `unverified` for Egypt).
- **Bundles**: combine items so the buyer gets a complete solution at a clear saving versus buying separately. Keep the bundle logic simple (starter/standard/premium); show the saving as a real difference. Watch margin per bundle.
- **Tiering (good/better/best)**: the middle option should be the one you want to sell; each tier must have a real functional difference.
- **Guarantees and risk reversal**: only promise what you can deliver and have written terms for: return window, inspection-at-door for COD, repair period, money-back conditions. State conditions plainly. A guarantee that you quietly refuse is worse than none.
- **Payment-plan framing**: translate to the monthly amount only when the plan is real, state total cost, fees and who provides the financing; do not hide interest or fees; financial products have sector rules. In a COD market, offering a clear choice between cash on delivery and a prepaid discount can be a legitimate nudge if the discount is real.
- **Urgency and scarcity**: allowed only when true (real stock count, real deadline, real seasonal date such as Ramadan or White Friday). No countdown timers that reset, no fake "only 3 left".
- **Entry offers**: sample, trial, first session, small size. Judge them by conversion to the core offer, not by entry sales.
- **Price testing**: change one variable, run long enough to see purchases not clicks, and watch margin, not only conversion (see `measurement.md`, section 5).

## 7. Objection handling
Collect objections from chats and sales calls; answer each with: acknowledge, evidence, risk reversal, next step. Keep a one-page library.
| Objection | Typical root | Response pattern |
|---|---|---|
| "It's expensive" | Value unclear, compared with the wrong alternative | Reframe to cost per use or to the real alternative; show what's included; offer entry option |
| "Will it really arrive / is this a real shop?" | Low trust in online sellers | Proof of address, real photos/video, reviews, courier tracking, inspection-at-door policy |
| "I'll think about it" | Unresolved fear or no urgency | Ask what is unclear; offer a sample or a call; give a true deadline if one exists |
| "I can get it cheaper" | Price comparison without quality context | Compare like with like; state warranty, service, materials |
| "Is it original / halal / safe?" | Fear of counterfeit or compliance | Certificates, sourcing, batch details; no claim you cannot document |
| "Cash only, no prepayment" | COD habit | Offer COD with confirmation; offer an incentive for prepaid only if real |

## 8. Naming and tagline tests
Use for brand, product, campaign and offer names.
- **Say it**: can a customer say and spell it after hearing it once, in Arabic and in English? Check Arabic letter shapes and spelling variants (ة/ه, أ/ا/إ).
- **Search it**: is the handle available on the platforms you use; does a search show a clash or a bad meaning; can you get the domain.
- **Meaning check**: ask 5 native speakers from the target region what it suggests; check dialect connotations across Egypt, the Gulf and the Levant if you sell regionally.
- **Legal**: trademark search in the countries you sell in before printing or paying for ads (get a professional check; `unverified` in this file).
- **Tagline**: one idea, no jargon, translatable, true. Test by asking 10 people to restate what it promises, and which brand it sounds like. Do not adopt a tagline you cannot prove.

## 9. Arabic and English messaging adaptation
- **Message in the audience's own words.** Start from verbatims. Do not translate English copy; rewrite the idea in the audience's dialect. Egyptian for Egypt consumers; Khaleeji for the Gulf; MSA for formal, regulated, institutional or multi-country copy (see `../../media-buying/references/mena-and-egypt-notes.md`, section 4).
- Keep one dialect per asset. Do not mix Egyptian slang into MSA legal text.
- **English for Egypt** suits tech, B2B, premium or expat audiences; many Egyptian consumers switch languages by context, so ask which language customers use when they write to you.
- **Numbers and currency**: one numeral system and a clear token (ج.م or EGP) per asset; the price must equal the page price.
- **RTL**: put Latin brand names and URLs carefully, preview on iOS and Android, avoid trailing punctuation after Latin tokens.
- **Cultural fit**: respectful Ramadan/Eid tone, avoid using religion to sell; modest imagery norms differ by country.
- Always have a native reviewer approve; machine translation tends to MSA-formal tone and wrong register.
Examples (same idea, different register):
| Intent | Egyptian Arabic | English |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery promise | توصلك في خلال يومين في القاهرة والجيزة، ولو اتأخرنا بنرجّعلك مصاريف الشحن. | Delivered in Cairo and Giza within 2 days, or we refund the shipping. |
| Risk reversal | اتفرج على الشنطة قدّام المندوب، ولو مش عاجباك رجّعها من غير ما تدفع. | Inspect it in front of the courier; if you don't like it, hand it back without paying. |
| Entry offer | جرّب المحفظة الأول بسعر خاص، ولو عجبتك نكمّل. | Try the wallet first at a special price; if you like it, we go on from there. |
(Only use promises the business can keep.)

## 10. Positioning statement template
> For **[target segment]** who **[situation/need]**, **[brand/product]** is a **[category/frame of reference]** that **[primary benefit]**, because **[reason to believe / mechanism]**. Unlike **[main alternative]**, we **[key difference]**.

Checklist: segment is specific enough to find; category is one customers use; benefit is one idea; reason to believe is provable; difference is stated against a real alternative. Keep the internal statement plain; derive the public tagline from it.
